---
title: 'Enable OpenID Connect'
description: 'Connect Rootprint to any OpenID Connect issuer such as Keycloak, Okta, Entra ID, Authentik, or Dex so your team can sign in with your identity provider.'
---

Rootprint supports one OpenID Connect (OIDC) provider alongside password, Google, and GitHub sign-in. Anyone the issuer authenticates can sign in. Rootprint keeps no allow-list of its own for OIDC, so restrict access at the identity provider. [Authentication overview](/configuration/authentication) has the rules shared by every provider.

## Setup

<Steps>
  <Step title="Register a client at your identity provider">
    Create a confidential OIDC client (an "application" or "web app" in most providers) with the authorization code flow enabled. Register this redirect URI:

    ```
    https://<your-rootprint>/api/auth/callback/oidc
    ```

    Rootprint requests the `openid`, `profile`, and `email` scopes. Copy the **Client ID** and **Client Secret**.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Enter the issuer and credentials in Rootprint">
    In Rootprint, go to **Settings → Authentication**, then click **Configure** on the **OpenID Connect** row. Enter:

    - **Issuer URL**: the issuer as your provider publishes it, for example `https://auth.example.com/realms/main`. Rootprint appends `/.well-known/openid-configuration` itself.
    - **Client ID** and **Client Secret**: from the client you registered.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Save the configuration">
    Click **Save**. Rootprint fetches and validates the discovery document. If it rejects the issuer, it saves nothing and shows why. On success, a **Continue with SSO** button appears on the sign-in page.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Issuer requirements

Rootprint rejects the issuer and saves nothing unless:

- The issuer URL and the discovery endpoints use `https://`. Plain `http://` works only on a private network: loopback, private IP ranges, single-label hostnames such as `dex`, and names ending in `.local` or `.internal`. An `https://` issuer cannot name an `http://` endpoint.
- The discovery document is reachable JSON with `authorization_endpoint`, `token_endpoint`, and `jwks_uri`, and its `issuer` field matches the URL you entered (a trailing slash is ignored).
- If the document lists `code_challenge_methods_supported`, it includes `S256`. If it lists `token_endpoint_auth_methods_supported`, it includes `client_secret_basic` or `client_secret_post`.

## Changing the issuer or client ID

Changing the **Issuer URL** or **Client ID** changes the identity behind every linked account. Rootprint unlinks every OpenID Connect account and signs those users out. They re-link by email on their next sign-in, or an admin can [reset their password](/configuration/users#reset-a-password). Rotating only the client secret unlinks nobody.

## Issuer unreachable at boot

If discovery fails while Rootprint starts, the **Continue with SSO** button stays hidden until a retry succeeds. `OIDC_RETRY_MS` and `OIDC_DISCOVERY_TIMEOUT_MS` set the retry interval and the fetch timeout. See [Environment variables](/configuration/environment-variables#optional).
